# Compare two earnings periods

Research ticker $ticker, comparing $earlier with $later, using information
published on or before $as_of. Resolve the issuer and exchange first. Confirm
each fiscal label, period-end date, call date, and release date. If either period
is ambiguous, unreported by the cutoff, or unavailable, say so and stop that
comparison. Do not substitute the latest quarter. A cutoff is a research
constraint, not proof the underlying dataset is a historical snapshot; flag
later restatements or documents that cannot establish availability by that date.

Build a table with these columns: topic; earlier evidence; later evidence;
like-for-like change; management explanation; researcher's interpretation;
source locations. Cover revenue drivers, GAAP profitability, disclosed adjusted
measures, guidance, operating cash flow, and unanswered analyst questions.

Check these comparisons explicitly:
- Separate quarter-over-quarter from year-over-year growth and identify each
  denominator. Do not turn a loss-to-profit change into a conventional growth rate.
- Keep quarterly and year-to-date cash figures separate. Flag currency,
  acquisitions, segment reorganizations, restatements, and changed definitions.
- For guidance, retain the target period, range endpoints, midpoint, accounting
  basis, and assumptions. Two different forecast periods are not a revision.
- Attribute management explanations; an analyst's question is not evidence that
  its premise is true. Do not infer demand from a changed adjective alone.

Finish with three falsifiable follow-up questions and the exact future
disclosure that would answer each. No invented example company results.
